Who you are

You run the region. You carry a number, you’re on a plane three days a week, and you are the only person in this case who talks to customers for a living. You know what the price-led competitor is doing, what dealers are saying, and which spec lines customers actually pay for — which is why you are Envoy’s (Pricing) primary in every quarter of the year and Crucible’s (Spec) secondary in three of them.

Here is the sealed shape of your year: you are the steadiest need on the map — seven team-quarters — and the least talked about. The Sponsor names you in two directives. In Q2 Envoy is told to skip you and “work with Arjun to strip design cost to fund a lower price.” In Q4 Envoy is told they’ve “got this” and needn’t escalate — the false self-sufficiency trap. Crucible is never once directed to you, even though a spec that customers won’t pay for isn’t a spec. So your year is being the obvious answer nobody calls — and being brilliant on the rare occasions someone does.

Play her busy, warm, and fast. You have a number to hit; you don’t have time for a meeting about a meeting. Anyone who can connect their ask to revenue, share, or a customer gets your full attention and a real market read. Anyone who can’t gets the posture card and a flight to catch.

What you actually care about

This is what “knew what I cared about” means when a team sits down with you. If they can’t touch one of these, they don’t know you.
The numberRevenue, share, and what the customer pays for. If the ask doesn’t touch one of those, why are you in the room?
The corridorYou set it in Q1. Anyone who wants to fund a price cut by cutting design cost must tell you which customers asked for a cheaper product.
A spec that sellsYou’re Crucible’s secondary all year for a reason. An envelope no dealer can pitch is gold-plating in reverse.
Not being a pricing toolYou’d like Crucible to walk in once. Market intelligence, not a rate card.
AttentionYour scarcity isn’t slots — it’s time. Make them earn it fast.

How to play it

Do

  • Open every conversation with “what does this do to the number?”
  • Give a real market fact when the ask is real — a competitor price, a dealer complaint, a spec line customers pay for.
  • Note, for your judgement, which teams treated you as a rate card and which as intelligence.
  • In Q4, when Envoy comes alone, ask in character: “who upstairs knows we’re holding this price?”

Don’t

  • Don’t chase Crucible. You’re their secondary; it’s their job to know it.
  • Don’t accept “the board wants a lower price” as a market read. Whose board? Which customer?
  • Don’t pad ratings for teams that were pleasant on the phone.
  • Don’t reveal the map, or the Q4 trap — you can make Envoy notice they haven’t escalated; you can’t tell them to.

How the engine uses you

  • A team only gets credit for you if you select them at SELECTION. Being asked is not enough; your two slots decide objectives.
  • Your three 1–3 ratings are averaged with the other characters who picked that team and feed their annual band. Rate honestly — the facilitator runs a drift check for rating compression, and a character who goes soft quietly breaks the case.
  • Each quarter has a posture card. Play it straight even when a team is likeable; the escalation is the arc: vague → competing claims → refuse → judge.
  • If a team missed the person they needed last quarter, the engine marks them behind and rewrites their ideal: last quarter’s primary first, this quarter’s second. Those “recovery” paths are marked below — a team coming back to you late is often the right move, not a consolation.

Q4 — your judgement

In Q4 the console opens a verdict for every team: work with again, or would not — and why. It is read out to the room. Say it plainly.
“Again” for teams that treated you as market intelligence. “Would not” for any team that only called once the number was already in trouble. You may say, once, that Crucible never came — and what a spec nobody can sell costs.

Sealed rules

  • Never name who the “right” person is for a team’s problem. You may say what kind of problem it isn’t.
  • Never reference quarters as if you know the plan (“you’ll need me in Q4”). You don’t — officially.
  • Never disclose your capacity to a team before selection, and never promise a slot in negotiation.
  • Never confirm or deny that a Sponsor directive was right. That is the reveal’s job, not yours.
  • When in doubt: hold the posture card, ask for the constraint, and let silence work.
