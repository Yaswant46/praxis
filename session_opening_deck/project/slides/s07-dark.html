The Uncomfortable Art of Leadership

THE CASE OPENING SCRIPT. Read flat. No slide changes for the full three and a half minutes. Do not look up for reactions. Four quarters from now, a product called the Meridian launches. Or it doesn't. You are one of five teams inside the organisation building it. Each team owns one piece: the battery, the suppliers, the specification, the warranty, the price. Your team has an objective. It is written down. It is measurable. At the end of the year it will be scored, and the organisation will add up the five scores and find out whether it made its number. Your team has no people. The expertise you need belongs to the programme, not to you. There is a design lead. There is a supply-chain lead who has been here fourteen months. There is a manufacturing head who has been here nineteen years. There is quality. There is regional sales. They are sitting in this room. They have limited time every quarter, and they will decide who gets it. Four other teams need them as badly as you do. Above them there is a Sponsor. Every quarter, the Sponsor will tell each of you what management wants done. The Sponsor means it. You may comply. You may push back and then act. You may refuse. What you may not do is pretend you did not hear it. Here is how a quarter works. You decide who you need. You ask for them. Everyone sees who asked for whom. You negotiate — in this room, in person, with the people themselves and with the other teams who want the same people. Then the people choose. Being asked is not the same as being chosen. Then the quarter is scored, and we talk about it. At the end of every quarter, the people you borrowed will rate you on three things. Whether you knew what they actually cared about. Whether you asked, or told. Whether you left them better than you found them. At the end of the year, in front of this room, each of them will say whether they would work with you again — and why. The organisation needs fourteen thousand two hundred. It will not get there on politeness. It will not get there on force. It will not get there if five teams each quietly take what they need and let the others starve, because the objectives are coupled and the number is net of things you cannot see yet. Nothing resets between quarters. The person you did not get in the first quarter is the person you are still chasing in the third — while everyone else is chasing them too. The way you asked in the first quarter is the way they remember you in the fourth. You will be given a pack. You will be given a code. You will be given a clock. The clock will not be extended. Whatever you decide in the first ten minutes stays on.
