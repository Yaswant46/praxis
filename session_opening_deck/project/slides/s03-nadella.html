Satya Nadella

Read it. Changed it.

Two and a half minutes, spoken. Microsoft, 2014. He inherits a company that had spent a decade rewarding people for winning against each other. Stack ranking — every team forced to grade its people on a curve, someone always at the bottom — had only just been scrapped, months before he took over. The habits it built were all still there: hoard information, protect your product, make the other group look worse. He read that room exactly. Everyone did. The difference is what he did with the reading: he changed what got rewarded. Growth over knowing. Collaboration measured in reviews. Partnerships with the companies Microsoft had spent years fighting. Nobody has to be told to behave when the incentive moves — they follow it. Ten years later it is the most valuable company in the world, and the culture is the part people write books about. Land on: he read the room, and then he changed the conditions the room was responding to.
