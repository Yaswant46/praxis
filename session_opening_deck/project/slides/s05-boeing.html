Boeing 737 MAX

Read it. Stayed quiet.

Not one leader — a building full of them. The pressure was clear to everyone: match the competitor's aircraft, on the old airframe, without pilots needing new simulator training, because training costs the airline money and time. Engineers read that room precisely. A software system was added to make the plane fly like the old one; its authority was quietly widened; its existence was kept out of the manual. Internal messages later showed people had seen the risk and said so to each other — not upward, not loudly, not in a way that stopped anything. Two aircraft went down, October 2018 and March 2019. Three hundred and forty-six people. None of the decisions along the way was a bad person's decision. They were small, safe, individually defensible ones, made by people who had read the room correctly and decided the room did not want to hear it. Land on: everyone knew. Nobody said.
